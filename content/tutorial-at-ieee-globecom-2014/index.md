<p class="rtejustify">The goal of the tutorial is to provide the audience with an introduction to the concepts, motivation&nbsp;and state of the art of the Recursive InterNetwork Architecture (RINA), one of the most promising&nbsp;alternatives to TCP/IP. This tutorial is split into two parts: the first one will be dedicated to the theory and&nbsp;concepts behind RINA, while the second will explain real-world use cases that motivate its deployment and&nbsp;introduce the RINA stack under development by the FP7 IRATI and PRISTINE projects. To conclude the&nbsp;session a live demonstration of the IRATI prototype will be carried out.</p>
<p class="rtejustify">The first part will start by exploring the lessons to be learned from the past. Most of the shortcomings of the&nbsp;Internet architecture stem from early design decisions that were made at the infancy of computer networking.&nbsp;The talk will briefly examine these decisions and their consequences for today’s Internet. The tutorial will&nbsp;proceed with an introduction to RINA, guiding the audience through the concepts and theoretical&nbsp;underpinnings of RINA. It will start with an overview of the architecture, explaining how recursion&nbsp;generalizes to all forms of distributed computing. The talk will then analyze how RINA deals with naming&nbsp;and addressing, routing, data transfer, flow allocation, security and network management. The first part will&nbsp;conclude looking at other alternatives to TCP/IP and analyzing how they compare to RINA.</p>
<p class="rtejustify">The second part will initially focus on RINA deployment considerations. The goal of this initial talk is to&nbsp;provide answers to the questions of “how can RINA be deployed?” by introducing a few scenarios that&nbsp;motivate RINA adoption. The lecture will also address some of the different ways in which RINA could be&nbsp;seamlessly deployed interoperating with the current systems and technologies. Having examined deployment&nbsp;scenarios, it will be time to concentrate on implementation strategies. RINA can be implemented in multiple&nbsp;ways for different target hardware platforms in a variety of operating environments. This tutorial will mainly&nbsp;focus on the RINA implementation of the IRATI&nbsp;project, currently also being extended by the PRISTINE&nbsp;project. It will give an overview of the software architecture and design decisions, glancing at the engineering&nbsp;problems and solutions applied to provide the first full-fledged RINA prototype. The last lecture will be&nbsp;devoted to presenting and explaining the various experiments that the IRATI project has been carrying out&nbsp;with the RINA prototype using the infrastructure provided by the OFELIA&nbsp;project.</p>
